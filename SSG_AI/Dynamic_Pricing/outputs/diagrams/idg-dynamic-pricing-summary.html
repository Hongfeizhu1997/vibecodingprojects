IDG Dynamic Pricing — Solution Overview

B2B · LBPi · LBPe | Pre-Study: Adjustment Method Evaluation
Service Price = HW Unit Price × Pen Rate
Service price moves with hardware price. When HW price rises, service follows; when HW price drops, Pen Rate adjusts upward to protect service revenue from proportional erosion. IDG uses the same tiered lookup methodology as ISG, with 3 additional service-specific delegation dimensions.

Pen Rate adjustment follows the same tiered lookup approach as ISG, ensuring consistent methodology across both product lines while IDG handles additional dimensional complexity.
Recommended Approaches
⭐
Tiered — Rule-Based Lookup
  • 3–5 HW price bands, fixed Pen Rate per band
  • 6-dim matrix: Service OH / Tier / Sub Tier / BU / Country / GTM
  • Zero data dependency — deploy immediately
  • Same approach as ISG; sales team can memorize
  • Risk: cliff effect at band boundaries
⭐
Power Law — Elasticity-Driven
  • Single parameter k = price elasticity
  • Smooth curve, no cliff effect
  • Each Service Tier can run its own k
  • Requires Phase 1 data for k calibration
  • Risk: harder to explain to non-technical stakeholders

Business Effects Comparison
3 Adjustment Methods — Business Impact Matrix
Comparing implementation readiness, pricing behavior, and operational fit for IDG B2B

Tiered ⭐ PR ∈ {PR₁, PR₂, …, PRₙ} per band Instant Perfect ⚠ Cliff
  • Zero data dependency — deploy immediately with business-defined bands
  • Sales team can memorize a one-page lookup table; fully auditable

  • Cliff risk at band boundaries (e.g. $4,999→$5,001 drops PR from 28% to 22%)
  • Mitigate by aligning boundaries with natural product price gaps
 Recommended
Power Law ⭐ PR = a + b · x−k Medium Excellent Smooth
  • k = price elasticity — the single parameter business adjusts quarterly
  • k=0.3: mild (HW ↓20% → PR ↑7%); k=0.5: balanced (↑12%); k=0.7: aggressive (↑18%)
  • Each Service Tier can run its own k — premium support gets higher k for stronger protection
  • Eliminates cliff effect entirely while preserving clear business semantics
  • k calibrated from Phase 1 data via log-log regression + LOESS smoothing
 Recommended
Exponential PR = a + (b−a) · e−λx Medium Good Smooth
  • λ intuitively controls decay speed — "faster HW price drop → faster PR adjustment"
  • High-end PR keeps declining slowly — may underprice premium workstation services
  • Mid-range transition is uniform, lacking ability to target specific price zones
  • Best for broad HW price-span portfolios ($1K–$200K) needing smooth coverage
 Alternative
Tiered — Best for Immediate Deployment
Zero data required. Define 3–5 HW price bands, assign fixed Pen Rate per band. Sales team references a one-page table. ISG already uses this method — IDG reuses the same approach with 3 extra service dimensions. Ideal when speed and auditability are the top priority.

Trade-off: cliff effect at band boundaries (e.g. $4,999→$5,001). Mitigate by aligning boundaries with natural product price gaps.
Power Law — Best for Pricing Precision
Single parameter k controls everything. Smooth curve with no cliff effect. k=0.3 (mild) to k=0.7 (aggressive). Each Service Tier can run its own k. k is calibrated from historical data: log-log regression → LOESS validation → quarterly business review.

Trade-off: requires accumulated transaction data; harder to explain to non-technical stakeholders than a lookup table.
Decision Criteria
Choose Tiered if deployment speed and stakeholder transparency are critical. Works immediately with zero data. Choose Power Law if pricing precision and eliminating cliff effect justify the calibration effort. The two can coexist — Tiered as the baseline floor, Power Law as the optimized ceiling during transition.

Exponential is a fallback: same smooth behavior as Power Law but with less intuitive business parameters (λ vs. k).
