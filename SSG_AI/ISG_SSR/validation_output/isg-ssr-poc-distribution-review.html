4 · Where They Differ — The 14.8%

Mismatch breakdown

Of the 340 mismatches, the model almost always gets the warranty type right (only 21.5% cross to a different type). The two main error forms are wrong term duration and wrong SLA tier — both "adjacent product" confusions, not category-level failures.

Mismatch formCount% of mismatchesWhat happened
Wrong term duration 15946.8% Same type, wrong length — e.g. customer bought 60M, model said 36M
Wrong SLA tier 10831.8% Same type + term, but Four Hours ↔ NBD confused
Wrong warranty type 7321.5% Different category — e.g. PSP Server recommended as Premier
Reading it for the business: the model knows which product family the customer needs — 78.5% of mismatches stay inside the same type. The remaining gap is about telling apart near-identical products (36M vs 60M, Four Hours vs NBD), which is exactly what the next round of tuning addresses.

5 · What This Means — Where the Tuning Lands

Next steps

The distribution view points to the same three adjustments already identified in the analysis — they now map directly onto the visible gaps.

① Term duration P0

The #1 mismatch (46.8%). The 60M→36M direction (76 contracts) loses coverage the customer already bought. Fix: default to the longer term when uncertain — seller keeps override.

② PSP Server P0

Visible in the type comparison: AI under-recommends PSP (−1.6pp). Driven by only 7.1% training share. Fix: augment PSP data + modest PSP preference.

③ SLA tier P2

Four Hours ↔ NBD confusion is symmetric (51:57) — no directional bias. Fix: add hardware criticality + industry features, not a tuning tilt.

Bottom line: 85.2% of recommendations are an exact product match, and the overall distribution is near-identical to what customers actually buy. The 14.8% gap is concentrated in two fixable, adjacent-product confusions — term length and SLA tier — that the P0/P2 adjustments target directly.
