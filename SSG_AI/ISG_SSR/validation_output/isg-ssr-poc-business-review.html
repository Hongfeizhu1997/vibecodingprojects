The recommendation model demonstrates sufficient accuracy to move into a supervised pilot phase.
What worked well: Premier warranty (87.8% of volume) achieves 86.8% Top-1 hit rate. Model generalizes evenly across channels, segments, countries, and business verticals — no stratification bias detected. Top-2 escalation recovers 91.8% of all cases.
What needs attention before full rollout: The 14.8% miss rate reveals three systematic weaknesses: Term confusion (60M ↔ 36M), SLA misclassification, and poor performance on PSP Server products. These are fixable with targeted improvements — none are fundamental model failures.

Improvement Plan: 5 Action Items Before Full Rollout

These improvements are sequenced by impact and feasibility. P0 items must be addressed before expanding beyond Premier scope. P1 items are strongly recommended for the Pilot phase. P2 items can be addressed during Pilot.

P0 Improve Term Duration Discrimination

Affects 46.8% of all errors | 159 contracts
The problem: The model cannot reliably distinguish between 36-month and 60-month warranty terms within the same product. This is the single largest error category. In 76 cases, the customer chose 60-month coverage and the model recommended 36-month — a direct revenue loss.
Proposed fix: Treat warranty term as an explicit input feature — not just part of the label string. Add term-specific historical patterns (e.g., "Enterprise customers in this vertical typically choose 60-month coverage on Rack Servers"). Consider a two-stage prediction: first predict warranty type, then predict term duration conditional on type.

P0 Expand PSP Server Training Coverage

PSP Server Top-1: 68.5% | 162 contracts (7.1% of volume)
The problem: PSP Server is the premium warranty tier but the model treats it as a rare class. 71% of PSP Server misses are misclassified as Premier — the model defaults to the dominant class when uncertain. If PSP Server share grows in production, this gap will widen.
Proposed fix: Augment training data with additional PSP Server samples. Apply class-weighted loss to penalize PSP Server misclassification more heavily. The model already knows PSP Server exists (Top-3 covers 88.9%) — it just doesn't rank it #1 confidently enough.

P1 Separate SLA Prediction from Product Recommendation

108 contracts (31.8% of misses) | Right product, wrong SLA
The problem: SLA (24x7 4hr Resp vs Next Business Day) is embedded in the product label string and not optimized as an independent target. In 108 cases, the model picks the correct warranty product but the wrong service level — confusing "24x7 4hr" with "Next Business Day" on the same product.
Proposed fix: Add a dedicated SLA classification head with its own loss function. SLA is binary in this dataset, making it a straightforward prediction task. Features like customer segment, hardware price tier, and business vertical are likely strong SLA predictors.

P1 Implement Confidence Calibration

Confidence scores currently uninformative | All 2,296 contracts
The problem: The model's addon probability scores do not predict correctness. Low-confidence recommendations (avg prob 0.68) are nearly as accurate as high-confidence ones (avg prob 0.75). In production, this means the system cannot self-identify risky recommendations for escalation or human review.
Proposed fix: Apply Platt scaling or isotonic regression on the validation set to calibrate probability outputs. Alternatively, use the probability gap between Top-1 and Top-2 rankings as a confidence signal — a narrow gap means the model is uncertain between two options.

P2 Address Long-Tail Rare Cases

48-Month Term: 52.9% Hit Rate | 72-Month Term: 0% | Small volumes
The problem: 48-month warranties (17 contracts, 0.7% of volume) and 72-month warranties (2 contracts) are too rare for the model to learn reliable patterns. These will naturally improve as more training data accumulates — they are a volume problem, not a model design problem.
Proposed fix: No immediate model changes needed. During Pilot, flag these rare-term recommendations for mandatory seller review. After 3–6 months of production data, retrain with accumulated samples.

Recommended Pilot Approach

Recommendation: Launch Pilot with Premier warranty scope only (87.8% of volume, 86.8% hit rate). The model is production-ready for this segment today. Show Top-2 recommendations when the model ranks two options closely — this recovers 91.8% of cases.
