The recommendation model demonstrates sufficient accuracy to move into a supervised pilot phase.
What worked well: Premier warranty (87.8% of volume) achieves 86.8% Top-1 product match. Model generalizes evenly across channels, segments, countries, and business verticals — no stratification bias detected. Top-2 escalation recovers 91.8% of all cases.
What needs attention before full rollout: The 14.8% miss rate reveals three error patterns: Term duration confusion within the same warranty type (46.8% of misses), adjacent product confusion within the same warranty family (31.8%), and cross-type misclassification (21.5%). Improvement Plan: 4 Action Items Before Full Rollout

These improvements target the three error clusters identified above. P0 items address the largest error sources. P1 items improve production readiness. All are actionable within the Pilot phase.

P0 Improve Term Duration Prediction Within Each Warranty Type

Largest error source: 46.8% of all misses | 159 contracts
The problem: The model frequently confuses 36-month and 60-month warranty terms within the same warranty type. This accounts for nearly half of all misses. In 76 cases, the customer bought 60-month coverage and the model recommended 36-month — a product with significantly different value. The reverse (36M→60M, 60 cases) is a more acceptable upsell.
Proposed fix: Strengthen the term duration signal in the feature set. Warranty term is often correlated with hardware lifecycle (server refresh cycles, lease terms) and customer segment behavior. Add features that capture: hardware product generation/lifecycle stage, historical term preferences by customer segment, and term distribution by hardware price tier.

P0 Expand PSP Server Training Coverage

PSP Server Top-1: 68.5% | 162 contracts (7.1% of volume)
The problem: PSP Server is the premium warranty tier but appears in only 7.1% of training data. The model defaults to recommending Premier when uncertain about PSP Server products — 71% of PSP Server misses are misclassified as Premier. If PSP Server share grows in production, this gap will widen.
Proposed fix: Augment training data with additional PSP Server samples. Apply class-weighted loss to penalize PSP Server misclassification. The model's Top-3 covers 88.9% of PSP Server cases — it recognizes the product category but lacks confidence to rank it #1.

P1 Improve Adjacent Product Differentiation Within Warranty Families

31.8% of misses | 108 contracts | Same type+term, different SLA tier
The problem: Within the same warranty type and term (e.g., "Premier 36 Months"), the model confuses the two SLA tier variants — "24x7 4hr Resp" and "Next Business Day". These are distinct products in the catalog with different prices. In 108 cases, the model picks the right type and term but the wrong SLA tier. This is balanced: 51 cases recommend NBD when 24x7 was bought, 57 cases the reverse.
Proposed fix: Add features that correlate with SLA tier choice: hardware criticality (mission-critical servers → 24x7), customer industry (healthcare/finance → 24x7), hardware price tier (higher HW value → higher SLA), and subsegment (LE/PS → 24x7, SM/GA → NBD). The pattern is symmetric (equal errors in both directions), suggesting the model simply needs better SLA-tier signal rather than a directional bias correction.

P2 Address Long-Tail Rare Product Variants

48-Month products: 52.9% hit rate | 72-Month: 0% | Small volumes
The problem: 48-month warranties (17 contracts, 0.7%) and 72-month warranties (2 contracts) are too rare for the model to learn reliable patterns. These will naturally improve as more training data accumulates.
Proposed fix: No immediate model changes needed. During Pilot, flag rare-term product recommendations for mandatory seller review. After 3–6 months of production data accumulation, retrain with the expanded dataset.
